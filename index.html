Adventure Begins Where Roads End

Mountaineering is the sport and activity of climbing mountains. It combines physical endurance, technical skills, planning, courage and respect for nature.

From beginners exploring their first trail to experienced climbers attempting the world's highest peaks, mountaineering offers unique challenges and unforgettable experiences.

Explore

Types & Styles

Mountaineering includes several climbing disciplines and styles.

Hiking

Walking through mountain trails and natural landscapes, generally without technical climbing equipment.

Ice Climbing

Climbing frozen waterfalls, glaciers and steep ice formations using specialized equipment.

Rock Climbing

Ascending natural rock formations using climbing techniques, ropes and protective equipment.

Expedition

Long-duration mountain journeys requiring planning, camps, equipment and experienced teams.

Rope Management

Navigation

Weather Reading

Movement

Survival

Mountain Sheltering

Protection from extreme mountain weather is essential during an expedition.

Mountain Huts

Permanent or seasonal shelters can provide protection, sleeping areas and basic facilities.

Expedition Tents

Strong four-season tents are designed to withstand wind, snow and cold temperatures.

Snow Shelters

In extreme situations, snow shelters can provide temporary protection from harsh weather.

Safety

Mountain Hazards

Understanding risks is one of the most important parts of responsible mountaineering.

Avalanches

Rapid movement of snow and ice can create serious danger on steep slopes.

Weather

Storms, heavy snowfall, strong winds and sudden temperature changes can be dangerous.

Cold Injuries

Frostbite and hypothermia can occur when the body is exposed to extreme cold.

Falls

Slippery surfaces and difficult terrain can lead to serious climbing accidents.

Stay Safe

General Guidelines

Important principles for responsible mountaineering.

01

Plan Your Route

Research the route, terrain, weather and difficulty level before starting.

02

Check Weather

Never ignore mountain weather forecasts and warnings.

03

Carry Proper Equipment

Always carry appropriate clothing, navigation tools, food and emergency equipment.

04

Never Climb Alone

Whenever possible, climb with an experienced partner or organized team.

05

Respect Nature

Follow Leave No Trace principles and protect mountain ecosystems.

06

Know Your Limits

Turn back when conditions become unsafe. Reaching the summit is never more important than returning safely.
